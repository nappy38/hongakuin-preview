二

The Temple

Hongakuin is a bekkaku honzan — a special head temple — of Kōyasan Shingon Buddhism, founded in 1190.

Legend tells that Kōbō Daishi sensed six Jizō bodhisattvas appear on this very spot. Centuries later, the monk Gyōkū climbed the mountain at the request of the poet Matsuyoi no Kojijū. As he chanted here, Jizō appeared in a blaze of light and said: Dwell in this place.

Over the centuries the temple has welcomed imperial princes, feudal lords and the great Kanō-school painter Kanō Tan'yū. In 1980, His Holiness the 14th Dalai Lama stayed here.

三

The Monks

Head PriestInaba Hōken

At nine years old, he lay unconscious with peritonitis. His father prayed to Kōbō Daishi: “I will give my son to Mount Kōya — please save his life.” The next day, the boy recovered. At fifteen he entered Mount Kōya, and has served the Buddha ever since.

He studied shōmyō — the Buddhist chant of Kōyasan's Nanzanshin school — under a former Chief Abbot of Kōyasan Shingon Buddhism, and for eight years trained some 640 young monks in its chanting.

“More than half a century has passed since I began serving the Buddha and Kōbō Daishi. Even now, I ask myself every day: am I worthy as a priest?”

四

A Night at the Temple

A temple stay is not quite like a hotel. From the moment you pass through the gate until you leave the next morning, let the rhythm of the mountain carry you.

Afternoon

3:00 pmArrival

Through the gate

Step through the gate and the sounds of town fall away. Settle into your room with a cup of green tea.

Late afternoon

Gaze at the garden

Five gardens surround the temple, including a rock garden by Shigemori Mirei, one of Japan's great 20th-century garden designers. Moss and stone change with every hour of light.

Evening

Dinner

Shōjin ryōri

Buddhist vegetarian cuisine handed down from Kōbō Daishi — seasonal, local ingredients prepared according to tradition, served before gilded screen paintings.

We are unable to accommodate individual food allergies.

5:00 – 9:00 pm

The bath

Unwind in the large or medium shared bath. Shampoo, towels, hair dryers and toothbrushes are all provided.

Night

Mountain silence

Night on Kōyasan is astonishingly quiet, lit only by lanterns — a lovely time for a gentle walk. The front door closes at 7:30 pm, but guests may come and go freely.

Morning

6:30 amMorning service

Morning service

The vice head priest and the monks chant in the main hall. Sit with them and let the voices of a thousand-year tradition begin your day.

Every morning at 6:30 · optional · about 45 minutes to an hour

7:20 amapprox.

Breakfast

After the service, a shōjin ryōri breakfast — a gentle, plant-based meal to begin your day on the mountain.

10:00 amCheck-out

On to Okunoin

After you leave, walk on to Okunoin, where Kōbō Daishi rests among ancient cedars.

Twice a day, at 6:00 and 10:30 am, monks still carry a meal to Kōbō Daishi at his mausoleum — a ritual called shōjingu. From Hongakuin, Gobyō-bashi bridge at the mausoleum is about a 36-minute walk.

Access & Good to Know

Getting here

  1. By trainNankai Kōya Line to Gokurakubashi Station, then the cable car to Kōyasan Station.
  2. By busNankai Rinkan bus toward Okunoin or Daimon; get off at Keisatsusho-mae (about 10 minutes).
  3. On footAbout 2–3 minutes from the bus stop, along a tree-lined approach.

FAQ

Can I stay alone?

Solo travelers are welcome. However, if no other guests are staying on a given night, we may be unable to accept a single guest. Please check availability by phone.

How do I make a reservation?

Stays can be booked online at any time via the "Book your stay" button on this page. You can also reserve by phone (+81-736-56-2711, 9:00 am–5:00 pm JST).

What are check-in and check-out times?

Check-in is from 3:00 pm, and check-out is by 10:00 am.

Is there a curfew?

No. The front door is closed at 7:30 pm, but guests may come and go freely after that. The mountain is very quiet at night, so please take care when you go out.

Is the food shōjin ryōri? Can you accommodate food allergies?

We serve shōjin ryōri, including dishes such as kōya-dōfu (freeze-dried tofu) and goma-dōfu (sesame tofu). We are unable to accommodate individual food allergies.

Can anyone join the morning prayer service? Is it required?

The morning service begins at 6:30 am in the main hall. All guests are welcome to join. Attendance is optional, not required. It lasts roughly 45 minutes to an hour.

Can international guests stay? Is English support available?

Yes, guests from overseas are warmly welcome. Our staff can assist you in simple English.
